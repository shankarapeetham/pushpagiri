History, Spiritual Roots & Sacred Guru Parampara

పుష్పగిరి క్షేత్ర మహాత్మ్యం (Treta Yuga & Antiquity)

Pushpagiri is situated along the banks of the sacred Penna River in Cuddapah (YSR Kadapa) District, Andhra Pradesh. Its sacred roots reach back into Treta Yuga and Vedic antiquity.

🌸 సంజీవని పర్వత దివ్య గాథ (Sanjeevani Parvat Legend):

When Lord Hanuman carried the sacred Sanjeevani Parvat from the Himalayas to revive Lakshmana during the epic battle of Lanka, a holy flower fragment fell from the celestial mountain and blessed this sacred earth. Hence, this blessed hill became eternally renowned as Pushpagiri ("The Hill of Flowers").

Revered across centuries as Pancha Nadi Kshetram (the confluence of five sacred rivers: Penna, Papaghni, Kumudvati, Varkala, and Mandavi), Pushpagiri uniquely hosts Sri Chennakesava Swamy (Vaishnava) and Sri Santaana Malleshwara Swamy (Shaiva) side-by-side on the same sacred hill, earning it the celebrated reverence of Dakshina Kashi (The Kashi of the South).

👑 రాజ పీఠం • THE RISE OF PUSHPAGIRI PEETHAM

శ్రీ విద్యారణ్య స్వామి వారి దివ్య పరివర్తన

For thousands of years, Pushpagiri was a powerful spiritual centre known as a prominent Shaiva Peetham throughout the entire Telugu-speaking region. His Holiness Sri Sri Sri Jagadguru Vidyaranya Swami transformed Pushpagiri Shaiva Peetham into Pushpagiri Sankaracharya Peetham.

Having realized the unique spiritual power embedded in the holy soil of Pushpagiri, His Holiness Sri Vidyaranya brought Pushpagiri into the eternal fold of Jagadguru Sankaracharya Peethams. This monumental spiritual transition took place as part of Sri Vidyaranya Swami's spiritual journey when he was the 12th Jagadguru of Sri Sringeri Sharada Peetham during the 14th Century.

For its unique significance as Raja Peetham, Pushpagiri was consecrated as:

‘శ్రీ శ్రీ శ్రీ జగద్గురు పుష్పగిరి శంకరాచార్య మహా సంస్థానం’

(Sri Sri Sri Jagadguru Pushpagiri Sankaracharya Maha Samsthanam)

Pushpagiri Peetham stands as an unbroken Vedic bastion, spreading Advaita Vedanta, patronizing Vedic pathashalas, protecting holy cows (Go Samrakshana), and guiding millions of seekers on the Sanatana Dharma path.
